    Why This Phrasebook Exists

    Reference guides for taking a clinical history in Hausa, Yoruba, and Igbo have circulated in Nigerian medical training for years—but no comparable resource for Idoma has been widely available, despite Idoma being spoken by millions of people across Benue State and beyond.

    Through public health awareness campaigns, one lesson stood out clearly: there is a profound difference between information that is merely available and information that genuinely lands in a language people trust. At the bedside, a history taken in a patient's own language is a more complete and more honest history—especially now as FUHSO's pioneer cohort steps onto the clinical wards.

    Alala (Orokam) & Everyday Clinical Terms

    Idoma is spoken in more than one variety. Entries tagged Alala (Orokam) record how the language is spoken in the Orokam area—collected following a medical outreach there where dialect differences became immediately clear at the bedside.

    In rural communities, patients also frequently use English words like "malaria" (usually meaning fever) or "ulcer" to describe symptoms rather than confirmed diagnoses, while terms like "pile" have no distinct Idoma word. Treat these as patient descriptions and ask follow-up questions rather than recording them as diagnoses.
